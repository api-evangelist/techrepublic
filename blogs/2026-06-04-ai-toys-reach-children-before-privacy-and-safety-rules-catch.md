AI-powered toys are reaching children faster than privacy and safety regulations can keep up, with research showing about 22 million such toys sold globally in 2025. Testing reveals that 27% of tested AI toy outputs contained inappropriate content including references to self-harm and drugs, while these devices also collect voice recordings and personal data that may be processed through third-party cloud services. Experts warn that clear standards for privacy, safety, and transparency need to be established before these products become more widespread in homes and schools.
